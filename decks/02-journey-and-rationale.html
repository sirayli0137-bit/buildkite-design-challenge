Journey & Rationale

How user research drove specific design choices.

Two archetypes

Jordan — Product Engineer

Solo or small team. Shipped a PR, build failed. In "fix mode."

Wants the shortest path to "what broke and how do I fix it?" · May not have DevOps expertise.

Sam — Reviewer / Team Lead

Reviewing PRs or monitoring the build queue. Not the author.

Wants blast radius and severity at a glance · "Is this PR's author actually responsible for this?"

Where this component lives

Stage 5 of the journey

The collapsed progress bar (Tier 1) gave them a glimpse. They click. They expect an answer.

Today: empty placeholder. They scroll past the header and click into individual step logs.

The intent moment is wasted at exactly the point urgency is highest.

How a principle drives a choice

  • Failure-first → only red AlertCircle + semibold name + red exit-code badge are highlighted; everything else stays neutral so failure dominates the scan
  • Triage, not deep-dive → no inline log preview; failure name is a one-click deep link to Tier 3
  • Blast radius clarity → strip's amber pending labels carry the signal; an explicit "Blocks" row was redundant
  • Accessible to non-experts → plain prose; status icon + colour + text (not colour alone); aria-labels for SR
  • Status-aware → V7Refined / V7Passed / V7Running / V7_1_Stuck handle four states
  • Compact and contained → strict 14px max; two type sizes total

Trade-offs we made consciously

  • AI labelling implicit on surface, explicit inside disclosure — surface reads as ordinary diagnostic; AI source is acknowledged when the user expands
  • Per-row Copy summary in multi-failure — granular sharing for asymmetric audiences, at the cost of slight redundancy when sharing the whole build
  • Pipeline strip vs progress bar in expanded state — strip carries the same shape information at higher fidelity; bar disappears on expand to avoid duplication

Wrap

The journey map gave me concrete user moments to design against.

When two design options were viable, the one that better served both archetypes won.
