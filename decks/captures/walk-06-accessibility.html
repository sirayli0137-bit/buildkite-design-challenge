Slide 6 — Accessibility

Six checks, each with a line of code

Not just "I thought about accessibility." Every item below points to something a reviewer can find in the code.

✓Screen readers hear status, not just names
Step links carry aria-label="${name}, ${getStatusLabel(status)}". Screen readers say "Test matrix, Failed", not just "Test matrix". Colour is never the only signal.
✓Changes are announced out loud
Copy summary uses a hidden <span aria-live="polite"> that reads "Summary copied to clipboard" on success. Silent changes feel broken.
✓Contrast meets WCAG AA
All body text is text-zinc-500 minimum on white (4.6:1). Amber and red use shades 700+ for readable contrast (4.5:1 minimum).
✓Expand/collapse buttons say what they do
Every expand button has aria-expanded. Decorative icons have aria-hidden="true". The chevron rotation backs up the ARIA state, doesn't replace it.
✓Focus rings on everything you can click
focus-visible:ring-2 focus-visible:ring-blue-600 focus-visible:ring-offset-2 on every button, link, and expand. Keyboard users always know where they are.
✓Real HTML, not nested divs
The pipeline strip is a <nav><ol>. Real <button type="button"> for actions, real <a href> for links. Tab order matches what you see.
