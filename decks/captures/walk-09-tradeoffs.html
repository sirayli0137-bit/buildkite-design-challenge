Slide 9 — Trade-offs

What I picked, and what I gave up to pick it

Every choice has a cost. Here's what I paid for each one.

What I picked

Tight for the common case 70% of failures are a single failure. A view that's tight for one beats a view that's spacious for five.
Buttons sit next to what they act on Retry sits next to the flaky tag. Preview fix sits next to the suggestion. Where you look is where you act.
Reused Buildkite styles Same Tailwind classes, status pips, button styles, type sizes as the rest of the codebase.
Stacks for multiple failures Same row pattern, same parts. One failure or five — same layout.
Refactored when it got messy Switched to a typed table when string-matching grew brittle. New failure types now plug in at one place.

What it cost

The numbers are mocked Flaky count, typical duration, cross-build count are all hardcoded. No real history API yet.
The "AI" is faked Suggestions come from a typed table, not a real AI. The table is the contract for when a real AI plugs in.
No real-user testing Three rounds of polish would have been worth one observation session with a Jordan and a Sam.
Strip gets messy on long pipelines Demo has 6 steps. Real Buildkite pipelines have 15–30. The strip wraps at that size.
A lot going on in amber Stuck, in-progress, flaky, blocked, AI Sparkles, cross-build banner. Icons and position keep them apart, but it's dense.
The point → Trade-offs are how you ship. The deferred items aren't gaps in the design — they're called out so the next person knows where the line is.
